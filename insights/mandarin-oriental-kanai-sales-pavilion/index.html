Mandarin Oriental Kanai Breaks Ground: What Buyers Should Ask at the Sales Pavilion

By Jorge Ángel Mejía · 2026-11-24

Mandarin Oriental confirmed on November 24, 2026 that it had broken ground at Kanai in the Riviera Maya. The official residences website lists a Sales Pavilion inside Kanai, and trade reporting says residential sales are underway. As of November 24, I could verify the pavilion’s listing and contact details. Its inauguration date and walk-in policy remain unconfirmed in the public sources I reviewed. Mandarin Oriental is targeting 2028 for the resort opening.

What has actually moved forward at Kanai?

The groundbreaking is a milestone for Mandarin Oriental’s first resort and branded residential development in Mexico. GIM Holdings is developing the project within the Kanai master plan between Cancún and Playa del Carmen. In its latest announcement, Mandarin Oriental described a resort with 120 rooms and suites and a residential component of 49 homes with three to seven bedrooms.

The operator also identified two amenities for residents, the Air Residents’ Club and the private Water Beach Club. Those are useful points of reference when comparing the project with other branded residences. The next step is to see how access, operating costs and the opening sequence are defined for the particular home a buyer is considering.

Why the Sales Pavilion matters

The pavilion is where interested buyers can start turning broad project descriptions into specific comparisons. A residence’s appeal may change materially with its floor plan, privacy, outdoor space, position within the development and route to the beach. Ask to see where the home sits in the master plan and what structures, landscaping or future phases may affect its outlook.

The official residences site lists the pavilion inside Kanai and gives a private inquiry contact. Before travelling, ask the sales team whether appointments are currently available, how entry is arranged and what materials they can share in advance. A public contact listing gives buyers a starting point. Access instructions and any opening event should come directly from the project team.

What I would ask before reserving

  • Which homes are currently available, and what does the written price sheet include? Request the floor plan, orientation, parking, any furniture package and the currency in which obligations are payable.
  • Which services and amenities are attached to this home, and which are billed separately? Ask for the current draft budget, the recurring charges it assumes and the rules that govern access for owners and guests.
  • What are the separate delivery milestones for the residence, residents’ clubs and resort? Ask how the purchase documents handle delays, interim services and any changes to scope.
  • What documents govern the operator’s role and the use of the brand? Ask which provisions apply on resale, which require developer consent and what happens to residential services if the operator changes.
  • Which design and specification details are contractual? Compare the presentation material with the latest drawings, finish schedule and purchase documents before treating any feature as part of the offer.

For a home that will be used seasonally, those answers are especially valuable. A family that spends several weeks in Mexico each winter may value dependable maintenance while away, straightforward arrival and consistent access to the facilities it actually uses. A buyer who expects to rent the home should ask for the rental rules, the service terms and the actual costs before assigning income to the purchase.

How I would compare Kanai with another branded residence

Start with how you expect to use it. Compare the time from airport arrival to the front door, the amount of privacy the home offers in peak season, the facilities you would use weekly and how those facilities are funded. Then compare the written ownership terms and delivery schedules. That gives you a basis for deciding which differences are worth paying for.

Kanai’s culinary and art programs add context to that comparison. Mandarin Oriental has named chef Grant Achatz and The Alinea Group for the resort’s culinary program and Bosco Sodi as art curator and design consultant. For a residential buyer, the practical questions are what owners will be able to access, when access is expected to begin and whether it is included in recurring charges.

The groundbreaking gives buyers a timely reason to examine the project. I would use a pavilion visit to come away with a specific residence to evaluate, a clear list of the documents still needed and a written account of what ownership includes. Those are the pieces that make it possible to compare Kanai fairly with another Riviera Maya home.
