The Founders' Coast: Who Built Tulum Before the Boom

By Jorge Ángel Mejía · 2026-10-24

Tulum was not built by hotel chains. It was built in three waves by founders who designed for themselves first, on land almost no one wanted.

1984–2001 · The pioneers

Ana y José (1984), often cited as the first formal hotel on Tulum's beach road. Then Zamas and Posada Margherita. Family cabañas, no grid power.

2002–2010 · The founder-designers

Amansala (2002), Coqui Coqui (2003), Azulik (mid-2000s), Casa Banana (2007), Be Tulum (c. 2008–10). Most were founded by Argentines: Nicolas Malleville, Eduardo "Roth" Neira, Sebastián Sas. Together they created the Tulum aesthetic.

2010–2015 · The brands

Hartwood (2010), Papaya Playa Project (2011), Casa Malca (2012), Gitano (2013). Food, design and nightlife took Tulum global.

One documented data point: in December 2001, the beachfront where Coqui Coqui Tulum was built sold for USD 170,000.

What it means for buyers today: you pay separately for the land, the architecture, the business and the story. The story is the easiest to overpay for.

Frequently asked

Who founded Azulik?
Eduardo Neira, known as Roth, a self-taught Argentine designer.
When did Be Tulum open?
Around 2008–2010, by architect Sebastián Sas.
Who created the Tulum aesthetic?
Owner-designers who were their own architects, most of them Argentine founders of the early 2000s.
